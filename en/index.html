No network · 01

Every phone passes the word on.

Each phone passes the message on to the next, 100 to 300 m in hand with a clear line of sight. Up to 12 hops, and 7 km with a LoRa radio. Every member becomes a cell tower.

No network · 02

Two subgroups too far apart? The internet links them.

One phone has signal, and the whole group benefits. Our relays keep messages for 48 hours, unable to read them.

No network · 03

Messages wait for whoever lags behind.

Whoever stayed behind gets everything once back in range. A signal that arrives late shows up without ringing.

SOS

Hold the SOS button for two seconds.

5 seconds to cancel. Then the SOS goes out with your location, to every Siflo phone in range and to your group, every minute. It doesn’t call the emergency services: if you have signal, dial 112.

In the field

When voice and whistle
are not enough.

Situations described by manufacturers and federations, and what Siflo changes. Sources under each example.

Canyoning · releasable rigging

Asking for more rope from inside the waterfall

A rope deliberately too short: the first one down has to ask for slack. Under the waterfall, voices don’t carry.

With Siflo: “Release the rope” reaches only the phone of the teammate at the anchor, with the name of whoever is asking.

Source: Petzl, “Securing the first person on rappel” (in French)

Ski touring · one at a time

Keeping in touch when the group spreads out

Steep slope: one at a time, in sight of each other. In the wind or a whiteout, sight is quickly lost.

With Siflo: “One at a time”, “Regroup”, “It’s cracking” arrive even out of sight. Keep the phone more than 20 cm from your transceiver.

Source: FFME, safety recommendations for ski mountaineering and ski touring (in French)

Caving · wet pitch

Getting through to those at the top of the pitch

Passed mouth to mouth at the bottom of a shaft, a message gets distorted, until it triggers a rescue for a leaf caught in the rope.

With Siflo: with a teammate halfway down, the message arrives word for word, with the sender’s name.

Source: ULSA incident report, Yorkshire, 2025

Festival · saturated network

Reaching the first-aid post when the tower saturates

A cell tower only takes so many connections. At a festival, calls and photos stop going through.

With Siflo: volunteers talk phone to phone, all the way to the first-aid post. Emergency services: 112.

Source: Bouygues Telecom, “Réseau en festival : pourquoi ça sature” (in French)

Climbing · at the crag

Not mixing up the calls

Out of sight, one misheard call, or one shouted by the next team, is enough to cause an accident.

With Siflo: “Safe” and “Lower me” reach your belayer alone.

Source: FFME safety letter no. 8 (in French)

Canyoning · teammate in trouble

Warning the person at the anchor about a problem they cannot see

Bulges, overhangs, wet rappels: a stuck teammate is invisible from above. Signs and whistles assume everyone knows the same codes.

With Siflo: you spell it out to the whole group, “Léa stuck under the waterfall, release”. Nothing to remember.

Source: French canyoning instructor handbook (in French)

Help

The questions
we get in the car park.

What is the range?

100 to 300 m between two phones in hand with a clear line of sight, 80 m measured underground. Every member relays, up to 12 hops, and a LoRa radio carries up to 7 km. Details in the Labs.

Does the map work with no signal?

Yes, once the area is downloaded, for free: map, relief, aerial photos and snow stay on the phone.

Where does the map data come from?

OpenStreetMap, IGN, swisstopo, Copernicus, Météo-France and its neighbours, Skitourenguru, Grottocenter. It’s all on the Sources page.

Do I need a network to use Siflo?

No. Phones talk to each other directly. The internet only links up a scattered group, as soon as one member has signal.

Does it drain the battery?

About 2% a day, measured on a Pixel 10. There is a battery-saving mode too.

Are my messages private?

Yes, end-to-end encrypted. Only the nearby channel and the public SOS can be read by all, and they are signed. Details in the privacy policy.

Can iPhones and Android phones be in the same group?

Yes, with or without a network. A nearby Android brings long-range Bluetooth to the whole group.

Does Siflo work on my watch?

Yes, with your phone nearby: Apple Watch, Garmin, Amazfit, and Wear OS soon. A Garmin inReach can write to the group too.

What is a LoRa radio for?

To reach further, with an off-the-shelf Meshtastic device. Messages, signals and SOS, no photos or voice notes. Details in the Labs.

What are verified rescuers?

Rescuers and guides whose phones are listed in a register signed by Siflo’s publisher, checked by the app. Everyone accepts, everything stays encrypted.

Why not WhatsApp or Signal?

Their simplicity and their encryption, with no internet and no phone number. WhatsApp belongs to Meta, which can change its rules.

Is it like BitChat?

Same principle off the grid, plus private groups, signals, the SOS, LoRa and a fresh key for every private message.

How much does Siflo cost?

Nothing, and it will stay that way. If it helps you out, you can leave a tip from its settings, on Android for now.

Who develops Siflo?

Siflo is developed by Guardia SAS, a French company, under its AI Diligence brand.
